Why spacecraft fire research matters.

Understanding a fire means understanding both the flame and the particles it produces. Spacecraft researchers need evidence about combustion, smoke transport and detector response under the conditions their instruments will encounter. A flame image, a detector signal and a particle model each reveal a different part of that story.

These NASA SoFIE images show controlled microgravity combustion experiments. They give the research a physical context; they are not validation data for AERO-PSI’s aerosol models.

01 / The challenge

The problem: measurements do not explain themselves.

A detector records a response. Understanding it requires a second set of questions: how did the particles evolve, how do they interact with light, and which experimental conditions were measured? AERO-PSI helps connect those questions without confusing a model prediction with an observation.

Different investigations measure different things. A voltage is not a particle diameter, and a shared material name does not make two runs comparable. Before drawing a conclusion, researchers must find the original values, understand the units and establish which conditions were controlled. Missing calibration or an unmeasured initial distribution can limit what even a convincing graph tells us.

02 / Connect the evidence

How AERO-PSI connects the pieces.

Begin with a question, inspect the NASA evidence, and use the models to explore a specific mechanism. Coagulation describes how a particle population can evolve; scattering connects an assumed population to an optical response. The gap finder checks which measurements and conditions are available for the question you asked.

Scientific intelligence helps summarize, rank and interpret that evidence. It can explain a selected row or calculated result, then help develop a study to investigate what remains uncertain. Source-linked findings, general scientific reasoning and model assumptions stay distinguishable, so a proposed explanation is never presented as a measured outcome.
